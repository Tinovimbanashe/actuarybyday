HarborBridge Model Evaluation

Selecting a model under staffing constraints while examining false negatives, language-group reliability, and the human consequences of threshold decisions.

THE DECISION PROBLEM

Model quality depends on the operating context

The case compared a decision tree, default logistic regression, and tuned logistic regression. The tuned model produced the strongest overall balance and stayed within staffing capacity, but it missed more urgent cases than the default model.

1

Performance

The tuned logistic model achieved 81% accuracy, 51.7% precision, 75% recall, and a 61.2% F1 score.

2

Capacity

The organization could review 30 cases per 100 requests. The tuned model flagged 29; the default model flagged 42.

3

Human impact

Tuning reduced false positives but increased false negatives from two to five, meaning three additional urgent cases were missed.

THRESHOLD TRADE-OFF

A feasible model is not automatically an acceptable model

Default logistic model

  • 90% recall
  • Only two urgent cases missed
  • 42 flagged cases exceed staffing capacity
  • More human review and more false alarms

Tuned logistic model

  • Best overall F1 score at 61.2%
  • 29 flagged cases fit staffing capacity
  • Improved precision
  • Five urgent cases missed
The client decision is not purely technical: manageable workload must be weighed against the greater harm of missing urgent people.

RELIABILITY

Aggregate performance concealed a language gap

The tuned model reached 91.7% recall for English requests but only 50% for Spanish requests. Four of the five tuned-model false negatives were Spanish-language requests.

The result does not prove a cause because the subgroup samples are small. It does justify investigation into translation quality, urgency phrases, missing deadline fields, and training-data representation before further tuning.

CASE EVIDENCE

Individual records revealed what summary metrics could hide

LEARNING BEYOND THE CASE

Different perspectives improved the analysis

Another team continued modifying the tuned model because it flagged 29 cases under a capacity of 30, leaving one review slot unused. I had treated the tuned model as a practical stopping point because it met the constraint. Their approach showed that satisfying a constraint is not always the same as fully using the available capacity, especially when one additional review might recover a missed urgent case.

Every group received the same case study, yet the analyses, areas of emphasis, and recommendations differed. Listening to those perspectives showed me how my own recommendation could be strengthened and why model governance should include multidisciplinary review rather than a single analytical viewpoint.

PROFESSIONAL REFLECTION

Technical judgment also requires humility

Use the full decision space

A threshold that meets capacity may still be improved. The unused thirtieth review slot was an opportunity to investigate a more protective operating point.

Seek different perspectives

Common data does not guarantee common analysis. Different questions, assumptions, and priorities can reveal overlooked risks.

Keep people in the system

Replacing a workforce with AI can remove original thought, contextual judgment, dissenting views, and innovative problem-solving.

Respond to questions deliberately

I learned not to become defensive. It is better to pause, answer carefully, ask for clarification, or say that I will verify a point and return with evidence.
